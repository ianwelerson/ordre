## Brand Positioning

### What Ordre Is

Ordre is a client communication platform for service providers. It gives every job a private, shareable status board that clients can follow in real time - no app download, no account creation, just a link.

### Who It's For

**Providers** - independent professionals and small service businesses: watch and jewellery repair shops, home renovation contractors, auto mechanics, bike shops, freelance developers and designers, appliance repair technicians, and anyone who takes on jobs for clients and needs to communicate progress.

**Clients** - anyone who has left something with a service provider and wants to know what's happening without having to call or text.

### Brand Personality

Ordre sits at the intersection of **precision engineering** and **tactile workshop warmth**. It is not enterprise project management. It is not a generic CRM. It is a _trust layer_ between service professionals and their clients.

The brand communicates three things instantly:

- **Professionalism** - the provider looks good using this
- **Simplicity** - zero friction for the client
- **Warmth** - this is about human relationships, not dashboards

### Creative North Star: "The Digital Atelier"

The experience should feel like a custom-built workbench: every tool has a dedicated place, the materials are premium and durable, and the atmosphere is quiet and focused. We move away from "software as a service" and toward **"software as a craft."**

We achieve this through editorial layout, intentional white space, high-contrast typography, and restrained use of decorative elements.

### Reference Products

These products inform Ordre's visual and strategic direction:

- **Linear** - precision UI, every element pulls weight, nothing decorative exists without function
- **Tally.so** - simplicity as a feature; generous whitespace, single-column clarity, no visual noise
- **Notion** - flexibility sold through visual modularity; modular card grids, neutral palette with purposeful color moments
- **Vivaldi** - bold personality; layout as brand expression, not just container

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- **Do** use tonal contrast as the primary depth mechanism - it's what makes the design feel physical rather than flat.
- **Do** lean into Geist Mono for small details (timestamps, board IDs, metadata). It reinforces the precision identity.
- **Do** use generous whitespace. If a screen feels busy, remove an element - don't add a container around it.
- **Do** use asymmetrical layouts on marketing pages for a custom, non-template feel.
- **Do** keep the Amber accent rare. It should feel like a deliberate highlight, not a theme color.
- **Do** use the alternating Snow/Warm Gray section pattern for visual rhythm on long pages.

### Don't

- **Don't** use gradients. Depth comes from solid tonal shifts.
- **Don't** use pure black (`#000000`). Always use Midnight (`#1A1D26`).
- **Don't** use blue. Ordre's trust signal comes from precision and warmth, not the default SaaS blue.
- **Don't** use shadows on resting elements. Shadows are earned through interaction.
- **Don't** add decorative borders, colored outlines, or thick dividers.
- **Don't** use more than two font families. Plus Jakarta Sans for headings, Geist for everything else.
- **Don't** use filled Material icons. Always use outline variants for consistency.
- **Don't** use star ratings, avatar photos, or social media icons in testimonials - keep them editorial.

## Voice & Tone

### Dashboard Copy

- Labels use Geist Mono uppercase for a structured, tool-like feel.
- Descriptions are concise and scannable - no marketing language inside the app.
- Timestamps are always in Geist Mono.
